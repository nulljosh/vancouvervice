## 1.27 (unreleased)
Joshua moves like a person: a real idle when standing, a jog on W and a sprint on Shift, each timed to his stride so the feet stay planted.
The North Shore mountains rise across the inlet: Grouse with its ski runs, Crown and the Lions, painted on Higgsfield, in place of the old grey domes. Rain hazes them and fog hides them.
Joshua fights and falls like a person: a real jab on F, a crouch-and-spring jump, and when a car hits him he goes down, lies there and gets back up.
Joshua draws and fires his pistol on the move, and on WASTED he takes the hit and falls where he stood before waking up at the hospital. Standing around, he shifts his weight now and then.
Brookswood, south Langley, on real streets (fast travel): every real building footprint (Overture Maps), houses with photo siding, shingled roofs, garages, front doors and driveways, photo Douglas firs, red cedars and bigleaf maples, photo lawns and asphalt, the Brookswood Village strip mall with its parking lots and real shop signs, Mount Baker and Golden Ears at their true bearings.
Brookswood landmarks built by hand from aerial photos: a 40 Avenue duplex row, the Apostolic Faith Church on 207 Street, the Chevron at 208 Street and 40 Avenue, and Brookswood Secondary with its tennis courts.
Walnut Grove on real streets (fast travel), every building from Overture footprints. Glover Road in Fort Langley gets 1920s wooden false fronts.
Fort Langley on real streets (fast travel): every building from Overture footprints, photo maples down Glover Road, the Fraser behind the village and the cedar palisade of the old fort measured off aerial photos. Highway 1 is in fast travel too.
Downtown on photo facades with storefronts on the street, the North Shore on the horizon, BC Place's dome and red band, Harbour Centre's saucer and Canada Place's sails. The highway got sound walls, exit signs with the real street names, a rippling Fraser and a Port Mann that rises on piers.
Drive to Vancouver: walk up to any car in traffic and press E to pull the driver out (one star), then take 200 Street north to Highway 1, over the Port Mann, along Hastings and into downtown. Brookswood and downtown are life size; the highway between them is the real road network, the Fraser and Burrard Inlet at half scale, so the trip takes about 18 minutes. Drive off one map and the next opens where you are, still behind the wheel with your speed, cash and stars.
Freeway traffic keeps to its own carriageway and lane, white lane lines and edge lines, a band of firs, cedars and maples along the highway, and Baker, Golden Ears and the North Shore on the horizon.
On the real streets Joshua stands in his own idle, jogs on W and sprints on Shift at the pace of his stride, instead of a sped-up walk.
People walk the sidewalks of Brookswood: scanned bodies at their natural pace, hips bobbing, arms at their sides. Everyday sedans in real car colours drive whole streets end to end, four times as many on 200 Street and 40 Avenue as on a side street.
The Apostolic Faith Church is cream stucco with tall bronze windows under a narrower raised sanctuary, matched to the aerial photo.
BC Place wears its red LED band and roof cables; Harbour Centre's two lit glass lifts ride up and down the tower.
Physics: a car at speed knocks you flat and you get back up; your car stops on other cars instead of driving through them.
Traffic thinks: cars brake and honk for you and for pedestrians, queue behind each other, pick a way at every intersection, turn through it smoothly and hold the right-hand lane.
People think: gunfire and explosions send the street running away from you, walkers wait at the curb for a gap and dodge a car coming at them, and cops chase you down the streets instead of through buildings.
The landmarks look like themselves. A real Gastown Steam Clock with four faces and steam, brick warehouses on Water Street, the Art Gallery's portico and dome, Rogers Arena in glass with its red ring, BC Place with masts, Granville Island's tin sheds and silos, Canada Place with taller sails, the BC Legislature with its green dome.
Real models from Higgsfield: the Gastown Steam Clock, Douglas firs through Stanley Park, and full street trees on the blocks around you (smooth leafy crowns farther out).
Photo facades: glass towers, concrete offices, a row of real shopfronts with awnings, Gastown brick, Victoria sandstone, Kits siding, real asphalt and sidewalk slabs.
Street furniture: lamp posts over every road, yellow hydrants on the corners, bus shelters with benches.
Fixes: getting busted or wasted mid car entry no longer drags you back into the car; reloading can't pay the night deposit twice; a swap or fast travel with Macs in the bag no longer sets off the heist; the McDonald's door no longer drops you in the pawn shop; Colin's car can't be stolen mid-tail; staff, Colin and dancers stay at their posts; the next job no longer starts under your feet; cars are no longer all rebuilt every time a cop spawns.
Real cars and bridges: the everyday car is a real 3D sedan, Lions Gate and Burrard have their towers, cables and trusses, the seawall rings Stanley Park, the Expo Line runs above Main Street, and Waterfront Station has its colonnade.
The whole story, start to finish: twenty jobs in three acts. Dmitri's forty grand ties it together, Colin launders Dmitri's cash through the McDonald's night deposit, and the running joke pays off: Joshua pawned his dad's hard drive years ago and gets it back from Colin. New scene jobs play when you reach the place (Dmitri's co-working space, Colin's place, the plan in Dad's garage), and the all-three jobs start at the garage with any hero.
Getting in and out of cars takes a beat: walk to the driver's door, thunk, slide in. Jack a car with someone in it and the driver ends up on the sidewalk yelling. You step clear of the door on the way out.
No more walking through tables, counters, bars or Dad's truck, and you slide along walls instead of stopping dead.
Club Velvet goes dark inside: a lit bottle wall behind the bar, light beams sweeping over a saturated dance floor, a bartender, and a crowd that dances.
Story key art in docs/img: the heist, Dmitri, the night deposit, Club Velvet, the tail, Kits Beach, the ferry, the plan, the getaway, the skyline.

Free roam between jobs. When a mission passes, the city is yours until you walk into the next job's yellow marker: Joshua's at the Steam Clock, Ben's at Dad's garage, Alexandre's in Victoria.

Standing people stand: staff, Colin and the dancers hold a natural pose instead of the crowd models' broken idle.
